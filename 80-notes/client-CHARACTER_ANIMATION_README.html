Animation guide: players, NPCs, gear and actions#

This guide is for someone new to Unity animation. It uses Unity 6.3, HDRP and the free Quaternius packs. By the end, players and NPCs move, fight, cast, flinch, die, gather and sit with real animations, and wear visible gear.

It's long because it explains why as well as what. Do it in order the first time. After that, the tables are the quick reference.

Part What
1 How animation works in Axiom (read once)
2 Unity's animation tools in 15 minutes
3 The player character, start to finish
4 Which clip goes in which state (exact Quaternius clip names)
5 Ability animations
6 Gathering, building, sitting (interaction animations)
7 NPCs: humanoids, monsters, vendors
8 Gear: armour and weapons
9 Test checklist, troubleshooting, what isn't wired yet
10 Guides and videos

Part 1: How animation works in Axiom#

The server decides, the client shows. The server says where everyone is, who hit whom and when a cast starts or ends. The client never decides anything about animation. It only plays the matching clips.

  server packets                          client
  ──────────────                          ──────
  movement (position, velocity, bits) ─┐
  W2CCastStart / W2CCastEnd           ─┼─> CharacterAnimator ──> Animator parameters
  W2CCombatEvent (hit, crit, miss,    ─┤     (one per player     ──> Animator Controller
                  killed)              │      and NPC)                (states play clips)
  W2CDoodadUseStarted / Ended         ─┘──> InteractionSkillPresentation (gathering)

CharacterAnimator sets parameters, the values your Animator Controller reacts to. You never write code. You build a controller whose states react to these names:

Parameter Type Set when
Speed float Every frame: ground speed in m/s (0 standing, 2.5 walking, 5 running; more with speed buffs)
MoveX, MoveZ float Every frame: sideways and forward speed relative to facing, ÷ 5. Only for a 2D blend tree
LocomotionDirection float Every frame: 1, or -1 while backpedalling (plays the run cycle backwards)
VerticalSpeed float Every frame: rising (+) or falling (-)
Grounded bool On the ground
Swimming bool In water
Mounted bool Riding a mount
Seated bool Passenger in a cart
Dead bool Dead, until revived
InCombat bool 6 s after any hit given or taken, or while casting. Also draws weapons
Casting bool During a cast that has a cast bar
SkillId int The ability that's playing
Jump, Land trigger Left the ground going up / touched down
Attack trigger A weapon ability went off, or an NPC swung
CastRelease trigger A spell went off (an instant, or the end of a cast bar)
CastInterrupted trigger Your cast was interrupted
Hit, Crit, Miss trigger You were hit, critically hit, or dodged (at most 4 a second)
Die, Revive trigger Killed / back on your feet

Every parameter is optional. If your controller doesn't have one, it's skipped, so you can start with a controller that only knows Speed.

Players and NPCs use the same system. An NPC gets a CharacterAnimator too, so the same parameter names animate an orc's attacks, flinches and death.

The model is looked up by a key. The database never names a prefab. It says Character_Default (the player body) or NPC_ORC (an NPC's ModelType). The WorldObjectPrefabs catalog maps each key to a prefab, and Tools > Axiom > Art & Assets > Asset Audit shows every key and which have no prefab yet.

Part 2: Unity's animation tools in 15 minutes#

Open these windows (top menu Window) and dock them somewhere you can see them:

  • Window > Animation > Animator: the controller graph.
  • Window > Animation > Animation: previews or edits clips. You'll rarely need it.

FBX files hold models and clips#

An .fbx file can hold a mesh, a skeleton and many clips. In the Project window, click the small arrow on an FBX to see what's inside. Clips have a triangle-shaped icon. Click the FBX itself, and the Inspector shows four import tabs:

Tab What you set there
Model Scale (Quaternius is already in metres: leave Scale Factor at 1)
Rig Animation Type: Humanoid for people, Generic for animals and monsters. Avatar Definition: Create From This Model
Animation One entry per clip: Loop Time, Root Transform baking, Mirror
Materials Extract Materials... so you can convert them to HDRP

Press Apply at the bottom after changing anything.

Humanoid: why one animation fits every body#

When a rig is Humanoid, Unity maps its bones onto one standard body (hips, spine, arms, head and so on). Any Humanoid clip then plays on any Humanoid character, a technique called retargeting. That's why the Quaternius animation library plays on the Quaternius bodies, and on Mixamo characters too. It's also how gear finds your hands and head.

Check a rig: select the FBX, open Rig, click Configure.... Every bone circle should be green. Press Done to leave.

Monsters (wolves, spiders, dragons) aren't human-shaped. They use Generic rigs, and their clips only play on that same model.

Clip import settings that matter#

Select the animation FBX, open the Animation tab, click a clip in the list, and check:

Setting What it does Axiom wants
Loop Time The clip repeats On for idles, walks, runs, swims, sitting, gathering loops. Off for jumps, attacks, hits, death
Root Transform Rotation > Bake Into Pose The clip can't turn the character On
Root Transform Position (Y) > Bake Into Pose The clip can't lift or drop the character On
Root Transform Position (XZ) > Bake Into Pose The clip can't move the character On
Mirror Flips left and right Off (handy to make a left-handed swing from a right-handed one)

Root motion is always off in Axiom because the server owns where you stand. A clip that tries to move the body fights the network and rubber-bands. Our importer bakes root motion for everything in Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Animations/ on the first import, but check older files.

The Animator Controller#

An Animator Controller (.controller) is a flowchart. Open it by double-clicking. The Animator window shows:

  • States (grey boxes): each plays one clip, called its Motion, or a blend tree. Click a state to see its Motion in the Inspector.
  • Entry (green): where it starts. It goes to the default state, shown in orange.
  • Any State (teal): "from wherever we are". A transition from Any State can interrupt everything. We use it for jump, death, hits and attacks.
  • Transitions (arrows): when to move from one state to another. Click an arrow to see its settings.
  • Parameters tab (top-left): the values from Part 1.
  • Layers tab (top-left): stacked graphs (see below).

Transition settings, in plain words:

Setting Meaning
Has Exit Time Wait until the clip reaches Exit Time before leaving. On for "play once, then go back" (an attack finishing). Off for "leave the moment the condition is true"
Exit Time 0.9 = leave at 90% of the clip
Transition Duration Seconds of crossfade (0.1-0.25 feels natural)
Conditions Parameter tests, all of which must be true: Speed > 0.1, Dead == true, a trigger firing
Can Transition To Self (Any State only) Leave off, or a held bool restarts its state every frame

Blend trees#

A blend tree is a state that mixes several clips by a number. Our Locomotion state is a 1D blend tree on Speed. At 0 it plays Idle, at 1.6 Walk, at 5 Jog (normal running) and at 8 Sprint, and in between it mixes the two neighbours, so speeding up looks smooth. Double-click the state to open the tree, and click Base Layer in the breadcrumb at the top to go back.

Layers and Avatar Masks#

The controller has two layers:

  • Base Layer: the whole body. Locomotion, jump, swim, ride, sit, die.
  • Actions: on top. Attacks, casts, hits. Its Avatar Mask limits it to the upper body, so you can swing a sword while your legs keep running.

An Avatar Mask is an asset where you click body parts green (affected) or red (ignored).

Animator Override Controllers: reuse a controller with other clips#

An Animator Override Controller (Create > Animator Override Controller) copies another controller's whole graph and lets you swap only the clips. That's how you give orcs a heavier swing without rebuilding anything: same states, different clips. Part 7 uses it.

Part 3: The player character, start to finish#

You may have done some of this already. Go through each step anyway and check it.

3.1 Folders and import#

The folder decides how a file imports (AxiomArtImporter). Put files in place before Unity imports them:

Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Characters/Quaternius/      body FBX files and hair    -> Humanoid
Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Animations/Quaternius/      UAL1_Standard.fbx          -> Humanoid, in place, loops by name
Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Gear/Skinned/Quaternius/    outfit parts               -> rig kept
Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Gear/Weapons/               swords, shields            -> static
Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Creatures/                  monsters (Generic)
Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Creatures/Humanoid/         humanoid NPCs (Humanoid)

The import rules only run the first time Unity sees a file. A file imported elsewhere and then moved keeps its old settings, so set the Rig tab by hand.

Use UAL1_Standard.fbx only. Delete UAL1_Standard_RM.fbx. "RM" means root motion: those clips move the body, which fights the server. The clip names are identical, so it's easy to drag the wrong one.

3.2 HDRP materials#

Quaternius materials show pink or white in HDRP. For each pack:

  1. Select the FBX files, open the Materials tab, click Extract Materials... and pick a Materials folder.
  2. Select the extracted materials and run Edit > Rendering > Materials > Convert Selected Built-in Materials to HDRP. If that menu isn't there, open Window > Rendering > HDRP Wizard and use the material upgrade button.
  3. Still pink? Select the material, set Shader to HDRP/Lit, and drag its texture into Base Map.

3.3 Make the body prefab#

  1. Select a body FBX in Art/Characters/Quaternius.
  2. Run Tools > Axiom > Art & Assets > Make World Prefab From Selected Model. It writes Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Prefabs/World/Characters/<name>.prefab, with the feet at the pivot and a CharacterSockets component on the root.
  3. Double-click the prefab to open it. In the Hierarchy you'll see the root (CharacterSockets) and under it the model child (it has the Animator).
  4. Check the facing. Select the root. The blue arrow (Z) must come out of the character's chest. If it comes out of the back, select the model child and set Rotation Y = 180. Rotate the child, never the root, because the game resets the root.
  5. Optional hair: expand the skeleton to the Head bone. Drag a hair FBX onto it, set Position and Rotation to 0, and nudge it into place.

3.4 Make it the player body#

Run Tools > Axiom > Art & Assets > Asset Audit. Find the row Character_Default and drag your prefab into its box.

  • One body per faction is optional: Character_Faction1 (Westmark), Character_Faction2 (Eastreach), Character_Faction3 (Pirates). Anything without its own prefab uses Default.
  • The prefab maker also registered the prefab under its file name (Superhero_Male_FullBody). That extra key shows as Unused and is harmless.

3.5 The controller#

  1. Run Tools > Axiom > Characters & Gear > Animation > Create Character Animator Controller. It creates Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Animations/Controllers/AxiomCharacter.controller, with every parameter from Part 1 and every state wired, but no clips yet. - Already made one before 10 Oct? Select it and run Tools > Axiom > Asset Pipeline > Upgrade Selected Animator Controller. It adds LocomotionDirection (needed for backpedalling) without touching your clips.
  2. Open the body prefab and select the model child with the Animator. Drag the controller into Controller, and untick Apply Root Motion.
  3. Upper-body mask. In the Project window, right-click and choose Create > Animation > Avatar Mask (in some versions it's directly under Create). Name it UpperBody. - Select it and expand Humanoid. - Click both legs, both feet and the small root circle under the feet so they turn red. Leave the body, head and arms green. - In the Animator window, open the Layers tab. Click the gear on Actions and drag UpperBody into Mask. Leave Weight 1 and Blending Override.

3.6 Fill the states#

Part 4 lists every state and the exact clip for it. To put a clip into a state:

  1. Click the state in the Animator window.
  2. In the Project window, expand UAL1_Standard.fbx.
  3. Drag the clip onto the state's Motion field in the Inspector.

Start small: Locomotion, Die, Dead and Attack. Press Play, check it, then fill the rest. Empty states are simply skipped.

Part 4: Which clip goes in which state#

These are the exact clip names in Universal Animation Library Standard (UAL1_Standard.fbx). Unity shows them as Armature|Idle_Loop and so on.

Base Layer#

State Clip Loop Time Notes
Locomotion (blend tree) Idle_Loop / Walk_Loop / Jog_Fwd_Loop / Sprint_Loop on Thresholds 0 / 1.6 / 5 / 8, in that order (Jog at 5, Sprint at 8)
Jump Jump_Start off
Fall Jump_Loop on
Swim Swim_Fwd_Loop on Swim_Idle_Loop is the treading-water version
Ride Sitting_Idle_Loop on Until there's a riding clip
Seated Sitting_Idle_Loop on Cart passenger
Die Death01 off
Dead Death01 off Holds the last frame (lying down)

Locomotion step by step: double-click Locomotion. In the Inspector you'll see four Motion rows with a Threshold column. Drag Idle_Loop into the first, Walk_Loop the second, Jog_Fwd_Loop the third and Sprint_Loop the fourth. Make sure Automate Thresholds is off and the numbers read 0, 1.6, 5, 8. Click Base Layer to go back.

There's no sprint key (as in classic MMOs, you walk or run). Normal running is 5 m/s, so Jog_Fwd_Loop is your run. The Sprint_Loop slot at 8 only plays when a speed buff or haste pushes you past normal speed, so a hasted character visibly runs harder.

The backpedal and strafe trick. The Standard library has no strafe or backward clips. With the Modern control scheme you strafe and backpedal, so the game turns the visible body toward the way you're moving and plays the run cycle backwards when you back up. That needs the Locomotion state's Multiplier set to the LocomotionDirection parameter, which the generator or the Upgrade menu does for you. (Click Locomotion: in the Inspector, Speed > Multiplier is ticked and set to LocomotionDirection.)

Actions layer (upper body)#

State Clip Loop Time Plays when
Empty (leave empty) Nothing on top, so the Base Layer shows. Never put a clip here
Attack Sword_Attack (or Punch_Jab when unarmed) off Strike, Cleave, Shield Bash and any weapon ability; NPC swings
Cast Spell_Simple_Idle_Loop on Held while a cast bar runs
CastRelease Spell_Simple_Shoot off A spell goes off (Mend, Fireball, the end of a cast)
CastInterrupted Hit_Chest off Your cast was interrupted
Hit Hit_Chest off You took damage
Crit Hit_Head off A critical hit on you
Miss Roll off You dodged. Roll is whole-body; it reads fine masked too

Optional extras from the same library#

Clip Good for
Jump_Land A Land state between Fall and Locomotion (trigger Land, exit time 0.8)
Sword_Idle A combat idle: a second Locomotion tree used while InCombat is true
Crouch_Idle_Loop, Crouch_Fwd_Loop Stealth, later
Driving_Loop Driving a cart (needs a Driving bool; not wired yet)
Idle_Talking_Loop Vendors and quest givers (Part 7)
Interact, PickUp_Table, Fixing_Kneeling, Push_Loop Gathering and building (Part 6)
Dance_Loop Emotes, later

Real strafe clips (later)#

If you get a pack with strafe and backpedal clips (UAL's paid tier, Mixamo, Synty and others), replace the Locomotion tree with a 2D one:

  1. Double-click Locomotion and set Blend Type to 2D Freeform Directional, with parameters MoveX and MoveZ.
  2. Add the clips at these positions: - Idle at (0, 0) - Run forward at (0, 1) - Run back at (0, -1) - Strafe right at (1, 0) - Strafe left at (-1, 0) - Diagonals at (±0.7, ±0.7) if you have them - Optionally, sprint at (0, 1.6)
  3. On the Locomotion state, untick Speed > Multiplier.
  4. On the Parameters tab, add a Bool named HasStrafeClips. Its value doesn't matter. Having it tells the game to stop turning the body and reversing the clip.

Part 5: Ability animations#

How the game picks an ability's animation#

When an ability goes off, the game looks at that ability's row in the Skills table:

  • AnimationParameter is set (for example Strike): it fires the Animator parameter with that name, so the state you made for it plays.
  • AnimationParameter is empty: a weapon ability fires Attack, a spell fires CastRelease. Every ability starts out like this.

So "giving an ability its own animation" means two things that must match exactly, capitals included: the name in the Skills row, and a parameter plus state in the Animator.

What fires the parameter (you never do)#

You don't call the parameter yourself. The game's code fires it:

  1. At login the World server sends every client the skill list, each skill's AnimationParameter included. This is why you restart the server after changing a Skills row: the client only learns the name from that list.
  2. Someone uses the ability. The server checks it and tells every client that can see the caster ("this character used skill 1").
  3. CharacterAnimator (Scripts/Gameplay/Characters/CharacterAnimator.cs) on that character gets the message, looks the skill up, and calls SetTrigger("Strike"). If the controller has no parameter with that name, it fires Attack or CastRelease instead. Abilities with a cast bar fire when the bar completes; instant ones fire straight away.
  4. The Animator sees the condition on your Any State arrow come true and plays the state. Unity clears the trigger by itself.

Because every client does step 3 for the caster, other players see your ability animation too, not just you.

The name has to match in three places:

Where What
Content Studio > Skills > the ability > AnimationParameter Strike
Animator > Parameters tab a Trigger called Strike
The Any State → state arrow > Conditions Strike

The state's own name doesn't matter to the code (only the parameter's does), but naming it the same keeps things readable.

Classes, shared skills and class skills#

All player classes use the same Animator Controller (AxiomCharacter). What makes classes look different in combat is their skills:

  • Shared skills (Skills rows with SkillsetId 0, like Strike) are one row used by every class, so they get one animation for everyone.
  • Class skills (Punishing Strike, Fireball, ...) are their own rows, so each one gets its own parameter and its own state.

The Animator doesn't know a character's class. Making a shared skill look different per class or per weapon needs code (a WeaponType parameter set from the equipped weapon is the planned way). Until then, one shared skill = one animation.

Walkthrough: give Strike its own animation#

1. The Skills row (do this first, or the game keeps firing Attack)

Open Content Studio (Ctrl+Alt+E) > table Skills > Strike. Set AnimationParameter to Strike. Commit (Ctrl+S). Restart the World server.

2. The clip

Pick the clip in the Project window (for example Sword_Attack from UAL1_Standard). On its Animation tab in the Inspector, make sure Loop Time is off (an attack plays once), then Apply.

3. The parameter

Open the controller (Art/Animations/Controllers/AxiomCharacter.controller) in the Animator window (Window > Animation > Animator). Parameters tab > + > Trigger. Name it Strike.

4. The state

Click the Actions layer in the Layers list (upper body, so you can swing while running; use the Base Layer instead for a whole-body move like a spin). Right-click an empty spot > Create State > Empty. Name it Strike. In the Inspector, drag your clip into Motion. A state with Motion None plays nothing.

5. The way in

Right-click Any State > Make Transition > click Strike. Click the new arrow and set:

Setting Value Why
Has Exit Time off Start the moment the ability fires
Transition Duration 0.05 Snappy, almost no blend
Can Transition To Self off Otherwise it restarts every frame while the trigger is set
Conditions Strike Without a condition it never plays (or always plays)

6. The way out

Right-click Strike > Make Transition > click Empty (or Locomotion if you put it in the Base Layer). Click the arrow:

Setting Value
Has Exit Time on
Exit Time 0.9
Transition Duration 0.1
Conditions none

Without this arrow the character freezes in the end pose.

When you're done, the graph shows one arrow from Any State down to Strike and one from Strike back up to Empty.

7. Test it

Press Play, log in, target something and use Strike. Keep the Animator window open and select your character in the Hierarchy: the Strike box flashes blue when it plays.

If it doesn't play#

You see Fix
The normal Attack swing instead The Skills row's AnimationParameter is empty, the server wasn't restarted after the commit, or the controller has no parameter with exactly that name (the game falls back to Attack when it can't find it)
Nothing at all, box never lights The Any State arrow has no Strike condition, or the parameter isn't a Trigger
The box lights but nothing moves Motion is None, or the Actions layer's Weight is 0 (gear icon next to the layer)
Stuck in the last frame No Strike → Empty arrow, or the clip has Loop Time on
Only the arms move Normal for the Actions layer (it's masked to the upper body). Put the state in the Base Layer for a full-body move
It plays twice Can Transition To Self is on

Channelled spells#

A spell held for the whole cast bar (a heal over a few seconds): make the parameter a Bool, not a Trigger. Its way out needs the condition <name> == false, with Has Exit Time off. The game holds the bool true for the length of the bar.

Branching on ability ids#

The SkillId int is also set every time an ability plays, if you'd rather branch on ability ids in a sub-state machine than add one parameter per ability.

Part 6: Gathering, building, sitting (interaction animations)#

World actions (mine, chop, gather, build, open, climb) are interaction skills: rows in Skills whose names start with Interaction:. Each has an AnimationParameter. The game sets that parameter for as long as the action's progress bar runs.

1. Name the actions in Content Studio (table Skills; find them by name, since ids can change):

Skill name contains AnimationParameter Clip from UAL1 Standard
Interaction: Gather, Harvest Gathering PickUp_Table (or Interact)
Interaction: Mine Mining Fixing_Kneeling as a stand-in, until you have a pickaxe clip
Interaction: Chop Chopping Sword_Attack with Loop Time on, as a stand-in
Interaction: Build, Repair, Batter Building Fixing_Kneeling
Interaction: Open, Pick up, Claim Interacting Interact
Interaction: Climb Climbing Push_Loop as a stand-in
Interaction: Sell trade pack Interacting Interact

Several skills can share one parameter. Commit, then restart the World server.

2. Add the parameters. In the Animator window, open the Parameters tab and add each as a Bool: Gathering, Mining, Chopping, Building, Interacting, Climbing.

3. Add a state per parameter, in the Base Layer (they're whole-body and often kneeling):

  1. Right-click > Create State > Empty, name it Gathering, and set its Motion. Turn Loop Time on for that clip.
  2. Any State > Make Transition to it: Has Exit Time off, Duration 0.2, Can Transition To Self off, condition Gathering == true.
  3. From it, Make Transition to Locomotion: Has Exit Time off, condition Gathering == false.

Bool or Trigger? Use a Bool when there's a progress bar (mining for 3 s). Use a Trigger for an instant action (opening a door). The game checks which kind you made.

Sitting on furniture: add Bools FurnitureSitting and FurniturePlaying, with states and transitions set up exactly as above. Use Sitting_Idle_Loop and Sitting_Talking_Loop. Without them, the game uses a simple procedural sitting pose.

Part 7: NPCs#

NPCs get the same CharacterAnimator as players, so Speed, Grounded, InCombat, Attack, CastRelease, Hit, Crit, Miss, Die and Dead all work for them. (NPCs don't swim, ride or jump, so those are never set.)

An NPC's look is its template's ModelType in Content Studio (table NpcTemplates). That key maps to a prefab in the catalog. Today most NPCs use NPC_ORC.

There are three kinds.

7.1 A humanoid NPC (guards, townsfolk, bandits, orcs on a human rig)#

  1. Put the model FBX in Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Creatures/Humanoid/. (Another Quaternius body or outfit works. Mixamo characters work too.)
  2. Check its Rig tab: Humanoid, then Apply.
  3. Select it and run Make World Prefab From Selected Model. Anything under Creatures/ gets: - a capsule collider you can click and Tab to; - the same layer as the Orc prefab, which is the layer clicks hit; - root motion turned off. The prefab goes to Prefabs/World/Creatures/.
  4. Open the prefab and check the facing (blue arrow out of the chest; otherwise rotate the child 180°, as in 3.3).
  5. Give it a controller. Either: - The same one as players: drag AxiomCharacter.controller onto the model child's Animator. The Humanoid clips just work. - Its own clips with an Override Controller: in the Project window, Create > Animation > Animator Override Controller, name it Guard, and set its Controller to AxiomCharacter. It lists every clip. Replace only the ones you want different (a heavier attack, a different idle), and leave the rest empty to inherit. Put Guard on the NPC's Animator.
  6. Give it a key. The prefab maker registered it under its file name. In Content Studio > NpcTemplates, set the NPC's ModelType to that name and commit. Or rename the key in Audit, for example to NPC_GUARD, and use that.
  7. Restart the World server, so it reloads templates and sends the new ModelType.

7.2 A monster (wolf, spider, golem: non-human shape)#

These use Generic rigs. Their clips only play on their own model, usually the clips that came in the same FBX.

  1. Put the FBX in Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Art/Creatures/. The importer sets Generic and keeps its clips.
  2. Make World Prefab From Selected Model.
  3. Make a controller for it. The simplest way is to run Create Character Animator Controller again, which gives you AxiomCharacter 1. Rename it Wolf, then: - Fill Locomotion with the wolf's idle, walk, run and run again (thresholds 0 / 1.6 / 5 / 8; wolves often use only three). - Fill Die and Dead with its death. - In Actions, fill Attack (its bite) and Hit. - Remove the Avatar Mask from the Actions layer (Layers tab > gear > Mask: None). Humanoid masks don't apply to Generic rigs. The attack will be full-body, which is right for animals. - Delete the states you'll never use (Swim, Ride, Seated, Cast...), or just leave them empty.
  4. Put Wolf on the prefab's Animator, untick Apply Root Motion, and set the NPC's ModelType as in 7.1.

7.3 Vendors and quest givers (they just stand there)#

Any humanoid prefab with a controller whose Locomotion first slot is Idle_Talking_Loop (an Override Controller is perfect for this). They never attack, so nothing else is needed.

NPC checklist#

  • [ ] The model faces +Z (blue arrow out of the chest) and its feet are on the ground.
  • [ ] It has a collider on the same layer as Orc.prefab, or you can't click it or Tab to it. The prefab maker does this for Creatures/.
  • [ ] Its Animator has a controller, with Apply Root Motion off.
  • [ ] The NPC template's ModelType equals the catalog key exactly (capitals count).
  • [ ] The World server was restarted after changing templates.

When an NPC dies, its model stays for 2.5 s to play Die, then the corpse takes over.

Part 8: Gear (armour and weapons)#

Gear comes from the Gear Visual Catalog: rows saying "item X looks like this model".

  1. Run Tools > Axiom > Characters & Gear > Gear Visuals (Database). It makes Assets/AxiomMMOKit/Axiom.Client/Resources/World/GearVisuals.asset.
  2. Add rows to Entries with +. Each row has: - ItemId: the item's id. 0 means "the default for this slot", used by every item in that slot without its own row. - Slot: Head, Chest, Legs, Hands, Feet, MainHand or OffHand. - Prefab: the FBX or prefab. - Attach: Skinned for clothes and armour that bend with the body; Socket for rigid things (weapons, shields, rigid helmets).

Armour (Skinned), from Modular Character Outfits - Fantasy#

Skinned gear must share the body's skeleton. If it doesn't, the Console says "bone 'X' isn't on the body's skeleton". Quaternius outfits and bodies match.

If an outfit part comes with a whole body attached, open it, disable the body mesh, and save it as a prefab under Art/Gear/Skinned/Prefabs/.

Weapons and shields (Socket)#

Slot First row
MainHand ItemId 0, a sword. Every weapon shows it until it has its own row
OffHand ItemId 0, or 25 Wooden Buckler, a shield

Weapons sit in your hands in combat and on your back out of it. SheathSocket changes the out-of-combat spot: HipRight puts it on the hip, and - keeps it in your hand.

Lining up a weapon (once per weapon):

  1. Press Play, equip the weapon and attack something so it's drawn.
  2. In the Hierarchy, find your player > body > hand bone > Gear_MainHand_<id>.
  3. Move and rotate it until it sits right, and note the Position and Rotation.
  4. Stop Play and type those numbers into that row's PositionOffset and RotationOffset.

Audit > Gear tab shows every wearable item and whether it has its own look, the slot default, or nothing.

Part 9: Test, troubleshoot, what's next#

Two-client test (ParrelSync)#

  • [ ] Both screens show the Quaternius body, not the capsule.
  • [ ] Idle, walk, jog and sprint blend smoothly. Jump and fall play.
  • [ ] Modern scheme:
  • A/D strafes, and the body turns toward the sidestep.
  • S backs off slowly, with the legs walking backwards.
  • The other client sees the same.
  • [ ] Strike swings; Mend casts; getting hit flinches; death plays and holds; respawn stands you up.
  • [ ] Your gear shows on the other client too.
  • [ ] Gathering plays its loop for the length of the bar.
  • [ ] An NPC walks, attacks, flinches and dies with animation.

Troubleshooting#

Problem Fix
Pink or white character Convert the materials to HDRP (3.2)
T-pose, sliding along The Animator has no controller, or the rig isn't Humanoid (Rig tab)
Runs backwards / moonwalks The model faces -Z: rotate the child 180° (3.3)
Runs sideways with the forward animation The controller is missing LocomotionDirection: run Upgrade Selected Animator Controller
Legs look wrong at normal running speed Locomotion thresholds out of order: Idle 0, Walk 1.6, Jog 5, Sprint 8
Walks off, then snaps back A clip has root motion. Tick all three Bake Into Pose boxes on that clip, and use UAL1_Standard, not _RM
Plays once, then freezes Tick Loop Time on the clip
Attacking freezes the legs The Actions layer has no UpperBody mask (3.5)
A creature's attack does nothing A Humanoid mask on a Generic rig: set the Actions layer Mask to None (7.2)
Can't click or Tab to an NPC Its collider isn't on the Orc's layer (7.1, checklist)
Gathering never animates The AnimationParameter spelling doesn't match the Animator parameter (capitals count), or the server wasn't restarted
Player is still a capsule Character_Default has no prefab in Audit
"bone 'X' isn't on the body's skeleton" That armour was made for a different rig
